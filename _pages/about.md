<summary>Biography</summary>
<div class="collapsible-section__content" markdown="1">

I am a PhD candidate in Management and Entrepreneurship at the CUNY Graduate Center and Baruch College’s Zicklin School of Business. I study how firms govern legally independent partners, coordinate interdependent activities across organizational boundaries, and decide what to bring under common ownership. I pursue these questions in platform ecosystems, drawing on theories of the firm to understand how digital technology shapes contractual governance.

My dissertation combines a systematic literature review, theory development, and quantitative analysis. The first essay reviews how different coordination demands shape platform governance. The second explains how digital architecture can strengthen contractual governance by making performance easier to document, verify, and challenge. This paper received a nomination for the PhD Student Paper Prize at the 46th Annual Strategic Management Society Conference and will be presented as a poster at the 2026 Corporate Strategy and Innovation Conference at the Wharton School.

My job market paper, the third essay, examines the conditions under which persistent coordination strain is associated with greater ownership-based sourcing. I assembled a longitudinal dataset of content additions across U.S. subscription video-on-demand (SVOD) services from 2016 to 2025, linking proprietary industry catalog records with historical corporate ownership and financial data. I find that platforms with greater prior dependence on outside suppliers tend to add more content from companies under common ownership following sustained turnover in externally supplied titles. The paper was presented at the 2026 Consortium on Competitiveness and Cooperation Doctoral Conference, the 2026 INFORMS Annual Meeting Job Market Showcase, and accepted for presentation at the 2026 Tepper School Colloquium on Artificial Intelligence for Business at Carnegie Mellon University.

I also study how publicly funded entrepreneurial ventures in Chile organize their activities while responding to market demands and public-sector accountability requirements.

My future research will examine how artificial intelligence influences competition among firms, platform governance, and firm boundary decisions.

My research has been supported by grants from the Zicklin School of Business and the CUNY Graduate Center.

Before pursuing my PhD, I worked in financial technology, nonprofit leadership, consulting, and energy and infrastructure projects across the United States, Africa, the Middle East, and East Asia. I hold an MBA from MIT Sloan, an MPA from Harvard Kennedy School, and an MPH from Boston University.

I teach Fundamentals of Management at Baruch College and received the 2025–2026 Zicklin School of Business Dean’s Teaching Award.

My letter writers are Elena Vidal-Branciforte (dissertation chair), Tobias Kretschmer, Evan Rawley, and Brian Silverman.

I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.
